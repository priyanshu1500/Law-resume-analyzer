# Tier 1 — Global authority resume guides

Research method: Exa web search + Jina page read via the `agent-reach` skill.
Notes below are paraphrased summaries of publicly published career-office
guidance, not reproductions of the source text.

---

### Source: "Resume Layout Guidance" — Harvard Law School (Bernard Koteen Office of
Public Interest Advising), hls.harvard.edu, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://hls.harvard.edu/bernard-koteen-office-of-public-interest-advising/opia-job-search-toolkit/resume-layout-guidance/>

- Header: current contact info; no job-objective statement anywhere on the resume.
- Education precedes Experience for anyone within ~5 years of graduation; list
  degrees most-recent-first; Latin honors (*magna/summa/cum laude*) italicized
  lowercase.
- "Honors" and "Activities" as two separate subsections per degree, capped at
  3–5 entries each — more dilutes the strongest items. Explain unfamiliar
  awards in one line.
- Don't list undergrad GPA unless requested or not otherwise reflected in
  honors. Don't list LSAT/standardized test scores.
- A "Thesis" and a separate "Publications" section (Bluebook citation format)
  are appropriate when relevant; note "(forthcoming)"/"(publication pending)"
  where applicable.
- Experience in reverse-chronological order. Don't feel obligated to list
  every job — curate for relevance; be mindful gaps this creates may need a
  cover-letter explanation.
- **Bullet style**: short phrases, not full sentences; start every bullet with
  an action verb; avoid passive constructions ("was responsible for running"
  → "Ran"). Longer descriptions signal higher priority to the reader, so scale
  bullet length to the experience's importance.
- Dates flush-right, consistent granularity (year–year is fine; exact months
  usually unnecessary); same format used throughout.
- Skills/Interests: language skills only if genuinely usable (may be tested at
  interview); common tools (MS Office, Westlaw/Lexis) don't differentiate and
  can be omitted; note specialized tools (GIS, coding) if relevant.
- No "References available upon request" line.

### Source: "Resume Workshop" (PDF) — Harvard Law School, hls.harvard.edu,
accessed 2026-09-05
<https://hls.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Resume-Workshop-for-Website.pdf>

- Resume = a condensed, chronological, easy-to-scan timeline, not a
  comprehensive life history.
- 1–3 bullets per experience; save longer treatment for the most significant
  roles; avoid vague descriptions.
- Limit to 1–2 pages; consistent formatting (e.g. "Sept." vs "September" —
  pick one); no graphics/pictures/colored fonts.
- Exclude: summary/objective section, references, lengthy publication lists
  (that's a CV, not a resume), high-school records, photos.
- Provided action-verb bank: Led, Supervised, Managed, Motivated, Facilitated,
  Enforced, Developed, Directed, Established, Generated, Increased,
  Engineered, Achieved, Resolved, Pioneered, Expanded, Organized, Created.
- Quantify results wherever possible (headcounts, %, counts).
- Explicitly address resume "questions a reader might have" (employment/education
  gaps, graduation-date changes) rather than leaving them silently visible.
