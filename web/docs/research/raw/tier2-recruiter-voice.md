# Tier 2 — Recruiter / hiring-side voice on legal resumes

Research method: Exa web search via `agent-reach`. Paraphrased, not reproduced.

---

### Source: "What Happens To Your Resume After You Hit Send" — Above the Law
(Wendi Weiner), abovethelaw.com, published 2026-03-04, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://abovethelaw.com/2026/03/what-happens-to-your-resume-after-you-hit-send-what-legal-recruiters-wish-youd-done-differently/>

Reporting from the 2026 NALSC conference (legal search consultants + Am Law
100/200 sponsors):
- Recruiters scan a resume in under 10 seconds; dense paragraphs lose them —
  structure and skimmability beat prose. They want practice area, level, job
  titles, notable employers, and key achievements at a glance.
- **AI-generated resumes are actively penalized** — recruiters report they can
  spot boilerplate, generic AI phrasing immediately and it costs the
  candidate the interview. (Directly relevant: the product's own generated
  suggestions must read as specific and evidence-based, never generic.)
- Common red flag: an unexplained history gap (e.g. selectively hiding the
  first 8–10 years of a career on ill-informed advice) — recruiters notice and
  ask.
- LinkedIn/resume mismatch (titles, dates) is a credibility red flag before a
  call ever happens.
- Prefer showing career trajectory/progression over a flat "laundry list of
  20 bullet points" of duties.
- Length norm cited: standard is **2 pages**, up to 3 with addenda (deal
  sheet, representative matters, publications) for senior/lateral candidates —
  this source is for practicing lawyers, not students, so the product should
  treat 1 page as the student norm (per Tier 1 + Tier 3 sources) and 2 pages
  as the post-qualification norm.
- Formatting: no "Etsy"-style templates with columns/graphics/color gradients
  for legal resumes — the profession reads those as unserious.

### Source: "3 Mistakes You're Making In Your Legal Resume" — Above the Law
(Wendi Weiner), abovethelaw.com, published 2021-08-31, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://abovethelaw.com/2021/08/3-mistakes-youre-making-in-your-legal-resume-and-how-to-fix-them/>

- Prefer a sans-serif font (Arial/Calibri/Helvetica) over Times New Roman/
  Garamond for on-screen digital readability — dated career-office templates
  from the 1990s–2000s persist and hurt readability.
- Centered section headings can aid scanability by drawing the eye to the
  page center (a stylistic recommendation, weighed as advisory not load-bearing
  for the rules engine, since it conflicts with some other guides' left-aligned
  convention — treat font-family/weight as the load-bearing ATS-adjacent rule,
  heading alignment as a soft style note only).
