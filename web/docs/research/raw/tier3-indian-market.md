# Tier 3 — Indian legal market sources (the actual target audience)

Research method: Exa web search via `agent-reach`. Paraphrased, not reproduced.
Note: searches for Bar & Bench / LiveLaw specifically surfaced fewer direct CV
how-to pieces than expected in this pass; the strongest India-specific how-to
source found was The Legal Catalyst (below), plus NLU Delhi's own recruitment
brochure. Both are cited accurately as found rather than mis-attributed.

---

### Source: "How to Draft a CV as a Law Student" — The Legal Catalyst
(Samyak Tiwari), thelegalcatalyst.in, published 2026-01-20, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://thelegalcatalyst.in/2026/01/20/how-to-draft-a-cv-as-a-law-student/>

- Indian legal CVs favor **substance over design** — recruiters prioritize
  legal exposure and clarity over visual flourish (echoes the Above the Law
  "no Etsy templates" point independently).
- Length by year: **1 page for 1st–3rd year students**, up to 2 pages for
  final-year students with substantial experience — more granular than the
  Western "1–2 pages" norm and worth encoding directly (India-specific rule).
- Layout: Times New Roman / Calibri / Garamond, 11–12pt body / 13–14pt
  headings, 1.15–1.5 line spacing, **PDF only** as the file format.
- Header should include name, professional email, mobile, city/state,
  LinkedIn (optional) — and explicitly should **not** include date of birth,
  gender, religion, or full residential address (an India-specific privacy/
  professionalism norm distinct from the Harvard guide, which doesn't address
  these fields since US resumes never included them to begin with).
- Career objective/profile summary section is optional, mainly useful for
  1st-years or students with few internships yet — contradicts the Harvard
  guide's "no objective section" for elite firm/clerkship targets; the rules
  engine should treat this as experience-dependent rather than a flat rule
  (present it as a soft option for low-experience profiles only).

### Source: NLU Delhi Recruitment Coordination Committee — recruitment policy
+ brochure, nludelhi.ac.in, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://nludelhi.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NLU_Delhi_Recruitment_Policy-Final.pdf>,
<https://nludelhi.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/RECRUITMENT-BROCHURE-Batch-of-2022-2027_compressed.pdf>

- CVs must **disclose** any pre-placement offer or upcoming assessment
  internship already secured through the placement committee — a formal
  Indian-market convention (disclosure norms) with no Western equivalent;
  flag as informational rather than a scoring rule (out of scope for a resume
  quality score, but useful context for the product's advice copy).
- Placement-cell messaging emphasizes moot court wins, academic/research
  conferences, and diverse internship spread (firms, chambers, judicial
  clerkships) as the credibility signals that "make well-rounded candidates" —
  validates weighting moot/publication/internship-diversity signals in the
  Practice Data / evidence graph.
- Batches now receive structured pre-placement training on professional
  conduct and client/firm expectations — a market signal that "professionalism"
  framing (not just substantive skill) is explicitly coached-for in the
  Indian market.

### Source: "The Law Student's Guide to Internships" — Lawctopus (Aditya Aryan
/ Arkapriya Ghosh), lawctopus.com, published 2026-03-12, accessed 2026-09-05
<https://www.lawctopus.com/the-law-students-guide-to-internships-from-nervous-first-day-to-courtroom-confidence/>

- Reinforces "internships matter more than marks" as the dominant peer
  narrative among Indian law students — supports weighting internship
  count/diversity heavily in the practice-fit and resume-strength scoring
  (already partially modeled in Practice Compass's `exp.interns`/`exp.tasks`).
- What seniors/supervisors actually look for from junior interns: curiosity,
  willingness to learn, basic professionalism — softer signals than
  substantive output for early-year students, useful context for how the
  product should frame feedback to 1st/2nd-years vs. final-years differently.
